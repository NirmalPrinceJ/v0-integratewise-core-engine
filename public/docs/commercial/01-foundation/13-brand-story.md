# 13 — Brand Story

Status: CANONICAL · Commercial OS
Owner: Product Marketing
Last verified: 2026-07-21

---

## Purpose

Who the brand is, what it believes, how it feels, and how it behaves in language and design. The brand story makes the strategy emotional without making it soft.

**Target audience:** Marketing, design, content writers, agencies; secondarily sales (tone) and product (voice in UI copy).

---

## 1. The Brand Belief

> **People deserve AI that earns trust — and organizations deserve to own their own truth.**

We are on the side of the person who got burned: who believed in AI, gave it real work, and got amnesia, half-finished tasks, and confident nonsense back. Our brand does not mock that disappointment or hype past it. We restore trust structurally: evidence before belief, approval before action, ownership before convenience.

## 2. The Brand Character

**Archetype:** the _Steward-Engineer_ — the person in the room who has actually built the thing, tells you the truth about it, and hands you the keys instead of keeping them.

| We are                                                                         | We are never                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Calm, precise, evidence-first                                                  | Breathless, hypey, "revolutionary"            |
| On the human's side                                                            | AI-worshipping or headcount-replacing         |
| Direct about limits ("the Twin is silent when it has nothing grounded to say") | Overpromising ("knows everything on day one") |
| Structural about trust (approval, audit, provenance)                           | Sentimental about trust ("just trust us")     |
| Plain-spoken                                                                   | Jargon-armored                                |

**Voice test:** read any sentence aloud to a smart, skeptical CSM at 8:55am before a QBR. If it sounds like a vendor, rewrite it. If it sounds like the colleague who already prepared the brief, ship it.

## 3. The Story We Tell (brand narrative, short form)

> You were promised AI that would change your work. What you got was another tab that forgets. So you did what everyone did — you demoted it, quietly, to writing emails. We built IntegrateWise for the day after that disappointment. One workspace instead of thirty tabs. A Twin that shares your bench and your context, prepares the hard parts, and shows its evidence. Truth that belongs to your organization — not to your tools, not to your AI. Nothing acts without your approval. This is what you wanted. How you wanted it to work.

## 4. Brand Language System

Canonical lines live in [18 Terminology §4](../03-positioning-messaging/18-terminology-guide.md). Brand-specific usage:

- **Doctrine line** ("Truth you own. AI you rent. Approval in between.") — the signature. Close keynotes, manifesto, about page with it. Never paraphrase.
- **Promise line** ("This is what you wanted. How you wanted it to work.") — hero-level empathy. Use where the reader's disappointment is the context.
- **Enemy naming** (Amnesia, Babysitting, Hallucination, Human API) — brand villains. Illustrated consistently; never introduce new enemy names casually.
- **The Twin** — always "your Twin," a colleague on the bench. Never "the AI," never cute mascot treatment, never anthropomorphized beyond colleague-hood.

## 5. Visual Direction (brief for design; system in `docs/designs/integratewise_design_system.md`)

- **Feel:** a calm workbench, not a command center. Light, spacious, evidence visible. Linear's restraint + Stripe's precision; never sci-fi glow, never robot imagery.
- **Motifs:** the bench (two chairs, one desk), the spine (a quiet vertical continuity element), provenance (visible source annotations as a design feature).
- **Approval moments are the brand's signature interaction** — design them like signing something that matters: deliberate, legible, satisfying.
- **Anti-patterns:** sparkle-emoji AI iconography, chat bubbles as primary motif, dashboards-with-everything screenshots, dark "war room" aesthetics.

## 6. Brand Behaviors (how the brand acts, not just speaks)

- We show the audit trail in public demos — including when it's boring. Boring is the point.
- We publish limits and non-goals (what the Twin won't do) as proudly as features.
- We never name competitors in advertising; we name _categories_ of failure (sales conversations may name names — [09 Battle Cards](../02-category-market/09-competitive-battle-cards.md)).
- Founder-led voice ([57](../11-marketing/57-founder-led-marketing.md)) is the brand's primary channel until Series A; the company account amplifies, never replaces, the human voice.

**KPIs:** brand recall of doctrine line in prospect interviews; voice-consistency score in quarterly content audit; % of UI copy passing the voice test ([20 PMM Guide](../04-product-marketing/20-product-marketing-guide.md) owns the audit).
