## Purpose

How to tell the Workspace — the category-carrying concept. The Spine story explains what we own; the Workspace story explains where work now happens.

**Definition (verbatim):** _The single environment where an organization's people and their Twins do their work._

---

## 1. The Story

Work used to live in one place — then software unbundled it into a hundred. Every job got an app; every app got a login, a truth, and a tab. The promise was specialization. The price was that _work itself_ — the connected, contextual, decision-making whole of it — stopped living anywhere. People became couriers between the places their work was stored.

The Workspace is where work moves back together. Not by replacing your tools — they stay, connected — but by giving the _work_ a home above them. Inside the Workspace, each person gets a Shared Workbench composed for their role and current focus; every workbench is projected from the same Spine of truth; every person works beside their Twin; every consequential action passes through governance. One environment. The tools become capabilities. The tabs become history.

**The claim under the story:** people should not work across dozens of disconnected applications. People should work inside one Workspace. That sentence is the category ([05](../02-category-market/05-category-definition.md)).

## 2. What the Workspace Is Not (boundary discipline)

| Not                                 | Because                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Another app beside the stack        | It's the environment _above_ the stack; apps become endpoints                                  |
| A portal or launcher                | Nothing launches elsewhere; the work happens here                                              |
| A dashboard suite                   | Workbenches are read-write operational surfaces — "Dashboards show work. Workbenches do work." |
| A chat product with tools           | Collaboration is on shared state, not in a transcript                                          |
| A replacement for systems of record | Tools stay; truth is assembled, not migrated                                                   |

## 3. The Composition Rule (why it doesn't become a junk drawer)

Every surface in the Workspace is composed from: **role + department + active focus + canonical work state + authority + available capabilities** (layer-model doctrine). Nothing appears because a vendor wanted a widget there; everything appears because the person's current work warrants it. This is the answer to "won't one workspace become another everything-app?" — composition is governed by work context, not by feature count.

## 4. Audience Renderings

| Audience     | One line                                                                                                                    |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Practitioner | "All your tabs had a baby, and it's calm: one bench with your accounts, your signals, your prepared work."                  |
| Executive    | "Your teams work in one governed environment instead of thirty apps — and the work compounds instead of scattering."        |
| IT           | "Tools stay; access is scoped; the workspace composes what each role may see and do, from one policy model."                |
| Investor     | "The workspace is the demand surface; the Spine is the moat. Own where work happens, and the stack reorganizes around you." |

## 5. Proof Beats

1. Morning open: _what changed / what needs you / what's prepared_ — the three beats live.
2. Cross-tool work without leaving: read CRM truth, see support signal, send governed email — zero tab switches.
3. Role composition: same account, CS workbench vs. sales workbench — same truth, different bench.

**KPIs:** % of user's workday inside the workspace (vision metric); tab-switch reduction reported in design-partner interviews; daily workbench opens/seat ([75](../13-operations/75-customer-success-metrics.md)).
