## Purpose

The single story of the product — told as a day in a user's life, then as a system. Every demo ([40](../07-sales/40-demo-script.md)), tour page ([53](../10-website/53-website-copy.md)), and onboarding sequence ([45](../08-customer-success/45-onboarding-playbook.md)) is a rendering of this narrative.

**Target audience:** Everyone who explains the product; the reference for what the product _means_.

---

## 1. The Narrative in One Line

> One workspace where your work already knows its context, your Twin has prepared the hard parts, and you decide.

## 2. The Day (the canonical user story — Maya, CSM, 8:55am)

**Before IntegrateWise:** Maya opens the CRM, the support desk, the billing tool, email, Slack, the usage dashboard, last quarter's deck, and a spreadsheet of her own making. Eight tabs to answer one question: _what's true about this account today?_ Forty minutes later she has a guess. She pastes half of it into a chatbot to draft the QBR summary; it invents a renewal date; she fixes it by hand. By 11am she has done the job her tools were supposed to do — again. She is the Human API.

**With IntegrateWise:** Maya opens her workbench. It opens on her work, not a feed: **what changed** overnight (a usage dip at Meridian, flagged with the evidence — which systems, which numbers), **what needs her** (renewal in 45 days; the champion who went quiet), and **what's prepared** (her 10am meeting brief, drafted by her Twin from the account's full truth — CRM, tickets, billing, the email thread where budget language shifted).

She reads the brief. It cites its sources — every claim traceable. She asks her Twin one question — _"what did we commit to in March?"_ — and gets the answer from the Spine, not a guess: the commitment, who made it, where it's recorded. She assigns the follow-up: _draft the renewal outreach reflecting the usage dip conversation._ The draft arrives grounded in what was actually said. She edits one sentence and **approves**. The email sends through her connected tools; the action, its evidence, and her approval land in the audit trail; the outcome enters the account's memory — because she approved that too.

It's 9:20am. The reconstruction ritual didn't happen. **The next piece of work started where the last one ended.**

## 3. The System (the same story, structurally)

```
Workspace            one place; tools stay connected, work stops being scattered
  → Shared Workbench   role-composed surface: her accounts, her focus, her authority
    → Human + Twin      same bench, same context; Twin prepares/proposes, Maya decides
      → Capability Execution   approved actions run across connected tools, audited
        → Organizational Continuity   truth, memory, and outcomes persist in the Spine
```

## 4. Narrative Rules

1. **Always start with the person, never the architecture.** Maya's 8:55am is the entry point; the stack diagram is the explanation, not the hook.
2. **The three beats are fixed:** _what changed → what needs you → what's prepared._ Every workbench, demo, and screenshot honors this order.
3. **Show the approval.** The signature moment of every telling is Maya approving a prepared action and seeing the audit trail. Governance is not a caveat at the end — it's the climax.
4. **The Twin never steals the scene.** It prepares and proposes; Maya's decision is the resolution of every beat.
5. **Silence is a feature.** When nothing is grounded, the Twin says nothing. Tell this proudly — it's the anti-hallucination proof and nobody else can claim it.
6. **End on continuity.** The story's last line is always some form of: _tomorrow starts where today ended._
