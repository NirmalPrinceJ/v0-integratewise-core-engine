## Purpose

The end-to-end journey — first touch to advocacy — with the emotional state, our job, owned assets, and exit criteria at each stage. Sales stages ([36](../07-sales/36-sales-methodology.md)) and CS plays ([44](../08-customer-success/44-customer-success-playbook.md)) are segments of this one journey; this document keeps them one story.

**Target audience:** Everyone customer-facing; the map the functional playbooks zoom into.

---

## The Journey at a Glance

```
UNAWARE → PROBLEM-AWARE → SOLUTION-AWARE → EVALUATING → PROVING → BUYING → ONBOARDING → ADOPTING → EXPANDING → RENEWING → ADVOCATING
   marketing ————————————→|←———————— sales ————————→|←—————————— customer success ——————————————→
```

## Stage by Stage

### 1. Unaware → Problem-Aware

**Their state:** lives the eight-tab morning; assumes it's just the job. AI disappointment internalized silently.
**Our job:** name the pain better than they can. The Human API, the Empty Context Tax, the Quiet Demotion — recognition, not persuasion.
**Assets:** founder LinkedIn narrative ([59](../11-marketing/59-linkedin-strategy.md)), manifesto + essays ([56](../11-marketing/56-content-strategy.md)), demotion-story content.
**Exit:** they'd say "that's literally my morning" — and follow/subscribe.

### 2. Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware

**Their state:** pain named; skeptical of remedies ("we tried AI").
**Our job:** introduce the category as the _shape_ of the fix — one workspace, a Twin on the bench, truth you own — before any product tour.
**Assets:** category page ([54](../10-website/54-landing-pages.md)), newsletter ([60](../11-marketing/60-newsletter-strategy.md)), webinar ([61](../11-marketing/61-webinar-strategy.md)).
**Exit:** website visit with ≥ 2 pillar pages viewed; demo request or Starter signup.

### 3. Evaluating (sales: Discover + Align)

**Their state:** hopeful, guarded; comparing against status quo, Gainsight, copilots.
**Our job:** discovery that quantifies _their_ numbers ([38](../07-sales/38-discovery-guide.md)); the Day demo with their tool names ([40](../07-sales/40-demo-script.md)); landmines planted ([09](../02-category-market/09-competitive-battle-cards.md)).
**Exit:** ICP ≥ 9, personas 1–3 engaged, POV scoped with success criteria + decision date.

### 4. Proving (sales: POV, 30 days)

**Their state:** the make-or-break window; users watching for "another tool" smell.
**Our job:** their tools connected day 1; the first real morning by day 2; one documented catch + measured prep-time delta by day 21; security review parallel ([42](../07-sales/42-security-faq.md)).
**Exit:** success criteria met in writing; economic buyer sees the realized-mini-ROI; commercial proposal requested.

### 5. Buying (sales: Commit)

**Their state:** convinced champions navigating internal friction (budget, security, legal).
**Our job:** make the internal sale easy — champion deck with their POV numbers, proactive security packet, clean paper ([32](../05-value-pricing/32-licensing-strategy.md), [43](../07-sales/43-procurement-faq.md)).
**Exit:** signature; kickoff scheduled within 5 business days.

### 6. Onboarding (days 0–30 — [45](../08-customer-success/45-onboarding-playbook.md))

**Their state:** optimism window; judging whether promises were sales fiction.
**Our job:** first value same-day (hydration before the kickoff ends); every seat's first morning in week 1; baseline metrics locked ([28 §4](../05-value-pricing/28-customer-outcomes.md)).
**Exit:** activation — ≥ 70% of seats with ≥ 5 approved Twin actions/week by day 30.

### 7. Adopting (days 30–120)

**Their state:** habits forming or failing; skeptics deciding.
**Our job:** cadence reviews on outcomes (not usage); rescue lagging seats via their peers' wins; first realized-value review at day 90.
**Exit:** north-star healthy (Approved Twin Actions/seat/week at target); P2 quoting outcomes internally unprompted.

### 8. Expanding ([46](../08-customer-success/46-expansion-strategy.md))

**Their state:** wedge team advocating; adjacent departments curious ("what's that morning brief?").
**Our job:** harvest internal pull — P3 champions the Sales/Ops workbench; same Spine, new benches makes expansion feel like turning on a light, not buying software.
**Exit:** second department live; tier upgrades where trust matured ([31 §4](../05-value-pricing/31-packaging-strategy.md)).

### 9. Renewing ([47](../08-customer-success/47-renewal-strategy.md))

**Their state:** rational check: did it pay?
**Our job:** no surprises — the renewal deck is the proposal ROI filled with realized numbers, plus the continuity argument (what the Spine now knows that nothing else does).
**Exit:** renewal ≥ flat, target expansion; multi-year where fit.

### 10. Advocating

**Their state:** the outcome story is _theirs_ now — the CSM who catches churn early, the VP with the board slide.
**Our job:** make advocacy effortless: case study built from their realized-value reviews ([64](../12-corporate/64-case-study-templates.md)), reference calls rationed and reciprocated, community roles ([62](../11-marketing/62-community-strategy.md)).
**Exit (perpetual):** referenceable logo; referral pipeline; speaker at our events ([63](../11-marketing/63-events-strategy.md)).

## Journey Design Rules

1. **One story, three owners.** Marketing hands sales a _problem-named, category-framed_ prospect; sales hands CS a _baseline-instrumented, expectation-true_ customer. Handoff artifacts are mandatory, not courtesy.
2. **Emotional continuity:** the journey's through-line is trust restoration (the Quiet Demotion undone). Every stage must _demonstrate_ governance, never just claim it.
3. **The moments of truth** (instrument these): first "that's my morning" · POV's first real catch · Day-1 hydrated workbench · day-90 realized-value review · renewal deck open.

**KPIs:** stage conversion rates ([73](../13-operations/73-marketing-metrics.md), [74](../13-operations/74-sales-metrics.md)); time-in-stage; moment-of-truth completion rates; end-to-end: first-touch → activated-seat velocity.
