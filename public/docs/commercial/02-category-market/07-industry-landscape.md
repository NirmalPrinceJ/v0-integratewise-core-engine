## 1. The Six Blocs

### Bloc 1 — Suite giants (Microsoft, Google)

Own the productivity surface and bundle copilots "free" into it. Strategy: AI as a retention feature of the suite. Structural limit: their AI's truth is their suite; cross-silo operational state (CRM + support + billing) is outside their model, and governance is a compliance checkbox, not architecture. **Implication:** never fight for the document/email surface; own the _operational work_ surface they don't model. Their bundling normalizes "AI at work" — free education for our category.

### Bloc 2 — System-of-record giants (Salesforce, ServiceNow, SAP, HubSpot)

Adding agents inside their records (Agentforce et al.). Strategy: defend the silo by making it smarter. Structural limit: they cannot sell truth-above-the-silo without cannibalizing the silo; their agents see their own data. **Implication:** we are their complement in the customer's eyes ("Salesforce is one of the tools we connect") and their long-term strategic threat — expect language adoption, respond with the category rubric ([05 §5](05-category-definition.md)).

### Bloc 3 — Model vendors (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind)

Own intelligence; racing into enterprise workspaces from the chat surface. Structural tension: their memory is _their_ memory — organizational truth inside a model vendor is exactly what 82% of the market fears. **Implication:** they are suppliers, not competitors, in our architecture — provider-agnostic by doctrine; "AI you rent" names the relationship. Watch: any model vendor shipping org-owned, approval-gated memory would be the serious move (none has — their engagement economics forbid it).

### Bloc 4 — Work-management & knowledge (Notion, Atlassian, Monday, Asana, Glean)

Closest surface neighbors: they own projects/docs/search and are adding AI. Structural limit: their state is _self-authored content_, not operational truth from the org's systems; search (Glean) finds, but doesn't hold state or govern action. **Implication:** sharpest positioning discipline needed here — "the Spine captures what happens, not what someone wrote down."

### Bloc 5 — Automation & integration (Zapier, Workato, Make, n8n, iPaaS)

Own the pipes; adding "AI agents" on trigger-action rails. Structural limit: no canonical state, no memory of _why_, no collaboration surface. **Implication:** category to escape, partner to embrace — customer automation stacks are execution targets for governed handoff (Canonical Handoff Contract), not rivals.

### Bloc 6 — Vertical AI & CS platforms (Gainsight, Totango, Sierra, agent startups)

Own one department's moment. Structural limit: single-department, rules-heavy or autonomy-first. **Implication:** our wedge collides here first (CS) — "feed your CS platform better data; see what it can't see."

## 2. Cross-Cutting Forces

| Force                        | State (mid-2026)                                                              | Strategic meaning                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **MCP & open protocols**     | De-facto standard for tool-AI connection                                      | Our connector economics improve with every adopter; IW-Continuity-Bridge speaks the standard natively. Bet: MCP-first stays right.         |
| **Edge infrastructure**      | Mature (Workers, D1, Vectorize et al.)                                        | 97%+ gross margin structure; per-seat COGS $0.55–2.10 (FINANCIAL_MODEL) — a cost moat vs. VM-era incumbents                                |
| **AI governance regulation** | EU AI Act phasing in; oversight requirements spreading                        | Every new rule converts our architecture from preference to procurement requirement — regulation is our sales tailwind                     |
| **Capital climate**          | AI application layer well-funded; autonomy hype cooling toward accountability | Our "governed collaboration" thesis matches the correction; fundraising narrative leans in ([68](../12-corporate/68-fundraising-story.md)) |
| **Talent expectation**       | Workers expect AI at work; trust it less than ever                            | Adoption friction low, trust bar high — exactly our design point                                                                           |
