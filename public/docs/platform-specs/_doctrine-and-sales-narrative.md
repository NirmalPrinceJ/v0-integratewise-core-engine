Part I — The World That Broke
⁠
The promise that was never kept
Software was supposed to make work easier. Instead it made work fragmented.
Every tool you connect to — your CRM, your chat, your project tracker, your AI assistant — starts from zero. None of them know what the others know. Your context dies the moment you switch tabs. Your memory resets every time you open a new AI conversation. Your data is locked inside someone else’s schema, someone else’s platform, someone else’s business model.
So you do what every knowledge worker does today. You become the integration. You copy context from Salesforce into Slack. You re-brief your AI assistant from scratch every morning. You build the same understanding in three different tools because none of them share it. You spend your day not doing your job — you spend it being the human API between systems that should already know each other.
This is not a tooling problem. It is an architecture problem. And IntegrateWise solves it.
⁠
Three frustrations, one root cause
“I say the same thing to every AI again.”
No shared memory exists. Each AI starts blank. You spend your time re-briefing instead of building on what was already established. Every morning is Groundhog Day for your context.
“My context gets lost when I switch tools.”
Context is trapped inside each tool. Your CRM knows one thing. Your project tracker knows another. Your support desk knows a third. Nothing connects them. The understanding you need lives in the gaps between your tools, not in any one of them.
“I can’t move my data — I’m locked in.”
The data lives inside the platform, not with you. The moment you leave, your context vanishes. Every tool you adopt makes it harder to leave, not because the tool is good, but because your context is trapped inside it.
The root cause is the same every time: there is no shared memory layer. Tools connect to each other through point-to-point integrations or through you — the human doing the work of a bridge by hand.
⁠
What the industry is doing wrong
The current market solves the wrong problem at every level.
Automation players (Zapier, Make, n8n) connect apps. They move data from one place to another through triggers and automations. That is plumbing. It is useful, but it does not preserve context across time. A Zap passes a record; it does not remember why the record matters, what happened before it arrived, or what should happen next.
Enterprise integration players (Workato, Tray, MuleSoft, Boomi) go deeper. They orchestrate workflows across enterprise systems. But the intelligence is still in the workflow definition, not in the data. Change the workflow, lose the reasoning.
AI search players (Glean, Dashworks) index everything and let you ask questions. But they are readers, not actors. They can tell you what happened; they cannot propose what to do about it and wait for your approval.
Workspace players (Notion, Coda, Linear) give you a surface to work on. But the surface is disconnected from your operational tools. You still have to bring the context in by hand.
AI chat players (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) give you intelligence. But each conversation starts from zero. There is no persistent memory, no organizational context, no governance layer between the AI and your operations.
Connector platforms (Replit, Vercel) give you infrastructure to build and deploy. But they connect tools to code, not context to decisions. The developer is still the integration layer.
Every category solves a piece. None of them owns the layer between. The layer that carries context across tools, across time, across people, across AI providers. The layer that makes work continuous instead of fragmented.
That layer is IntegrateWise.

Part III — The Doctrine
⁠
Three sentences that govern everything
Truth you own.
The data in your workspace — your entities, your relationships, your history — belongs to your organization. It lives in the Spine. You can see it, manage it, export it. No tool locks it away. No provider owns it. The context stays with you forever.
AI you rent.
The reasoning layer — the model that reads your context and proposes actions — is replaceable by design. Today it might be GPT. Tomorrow it might be Claude, Gemini, Grok, or a model that does not exist yet. You swap models by configuration, not by migration. The AI serves your context; your context never serves the AI.
Approval in between.
Every action the AI proposes passes through a governance gate before it touches anything real. High-confidence actions auto-execute with a full audit trail. Medium-confidence actions surface on your desk for review. Low-confidence actions are discarded. You are always in control. The AI does not drift. It does not hallucinate into execution. It proposes within your boundaries and waits.
Truth you own. AI you rent. Approval in between.

Two doors, one Spine
The architecture has exactly two external boundaries, with the Spine between them.
North Plane — The API Gateway. Every consumer enters here. AI assistants over inbound MCP. Experiences over capability calls. External agents over the public API. The Gateway authenticates, resolves the tenant, routes to the right capability, and enforces rate limits.
South Plane — The Integration Router. Every outbound action exits here. The Router picks the right adapter, injects context, manages credentials, and monitors health.
Neither plane talks to the other except through the Spine. That is the invariant that makes everything else work: tenant isolation, governance enforcement, audit completeness, and storage swappability all depend on it.

Part VIII — OODA as Architectural Law
⁠
The original OODA loop
Colonel John Boyd developed the OODA loop to explain why some pilots consistently won dogfights. It was not about faster aircraft. It was about faster decisions.
OBSERVE → ORIENT → DECIDE → ACT
↑ │
└───────────────────┘
Observe — Gather data from the environment.
Orient — Analyze and synthesize against prior knowledge. Boyd called this the schwerpunkt — the center of gravity of the entire loop. Everything flows through Orientation.
Decide — Select a course of action.
Act — Execute. Then observe the result. The loop tightens.
Boyd’s core thesis: the side that cycles through OODA faster — and orients more accurately — wins. Not the side with more data. Not the side with more firepower. The side that understands faster and acts on that understanding before the opponent can react.
⁠
Why Orientation is everything
Boyd placed disproportionate weight on Orientation. Two pilots can observe the same radar blip. One sees a threat. The other sees a decoy. The difference is not in the observation. It is in the orientation — the accumulated context, pattern recognition, and prior experience that shapes interpretation.
⁠
The Spine IS the Orientation engine. Without the Spine, every observation is raw data. With the Spine, every observation is understood in context. This is why IntegrateWise exists — the platform does not automate decisions; it makes orientation so fast and so rich that the decision becomes obvious.

Observe — Connectors and Signals
⁠
The Connectivity Platform receives data through the Integration Manager. Every signal is stamped with provenance. The connector does not reason — it only observes.
Orient — The Spine and Context Assembly
⁠
The Normalizer transforms raw data into canonical entities. The Spine binds them to stable IDs. Context Assembly builds the full picture. Capabilities are resolved here — the Twin determines which Platform Capabilities are needed based on the oriented context. The user never sees this resolution.
This is the step that matters most. Two platforms can observe the same Salesforce webhook. Only IntegrateWise can orient it against the full context of every prior interaction, every relationship, every learned pattern, every governance rule.
Propose — The Twin
The Twin reads the oriented context — Entity360, timeline, memory, signals — and proposes an action. Not a raw output. A structured proposal with evidence, confidence, and a recommended course.
Govern — The Gate
≥ 0.85 confidence: Auto-approve. Execute. Audit.
0.70–0.85: Surface on My Desk. Human reviews.
< 0.70: Discard. Log for learning.
This is IntegrateWise’s extension to OODA. Boyd assumed the decision-maker was also the actor. IntegrateWise assumes the AI proposes and the human governs. The gate is non-negotiable.
Execute — The Integration Router
An approved action routes through the South Plane to the target tool. Entity360 context is injected before the call. No action touches the outside world without an approval token.
Writeback, Remember, Organize, Learn — The Extensions
These steps do not exist in Boyd’s original OODA. They are IntegrateWise’s critical addition.
Every outcome writes back to the Spine. The Continuity Engine extracts knowledge with confidence scores. Memory is classified into scopes (Personal, Work, Organization, AI). Relationships are updated. The next Orientation is richer. The loop tightens. The platform gets smarter the longer you use it — not because the model changes, but because the orientation improves.

Memory Triage — The Critical Intelligence
This is the piece that determines organizational continuity. Without triage, the Spine becomes a dump. With triage, it becomes wisdom.
MEMORY TRIAGE

Every piece of knowledge enters the Spine with a LIFECYCLE.

STAGE 1: HOT MEMORY (0-7 days)
→ Recent events, active context
→ Lives in fast storage (Postgres / KV)
→ Available instantly to every workbench and Twin
→ Example: "Sarah reviewed Acme Corp today at 9:14 AM"
→ Example: "Bug #8821 resolved 2 hours ago"

STAGE 2: WARM MEMORY (7-30 days)
→ Still relevant, accessed occasionally
→ Migrated to indexed storage
→ Available on query, not pre-loaded
→ Example: "Acme Corp health has dropped 3 times this month"
→ Example: "David approved 14 of 16 proposals in the last 2 weeks"

STAGE 3: COLD MEMORY (30-90 days)
→ Historical reference
→ Lives in R2 (object storage)
→ Indexed for search and pattern analysis
→ Example: "Q1 churn pattern: accounts with health < 60
at renewal had 73% churn rate"
→ Example: "Sarah's Q1 close rate was 34%"

STAGE 4: PERMANENT MEMORY (90+ days → forever)
→ Crystallized organizational knowledge
→ Patterns that have proven true over time
→ Behavioral models that are stable
→ Decisions that define how the org operates
→ Example: "Enterprise accounts always get auto-escalation"
→ Example: "Customer health correlates with bug resolution time"
→ Example: "Executive QBRs happen quarterly, always with
health and pipeline data"

STAGE 5: DISCARDED
→ Temporary noise
→ One-off events that don't repeat
→ Outdated information superseded by new data
→ Example: "Slack message 'lunch at noon?' — no business value"
→ Example: "Draft email never sent — superseded by final version"

THE TRIAGE RULES:
→ Repeated patterns STRENGTHEN (move toward permanent)
→ One-off events DECAY (move toward discarded)
→ User-confirmed knowledge ELEVATES (instantly becomes trusted)
→ AI-observed patterns must PROVE themselves over time
→ Organizational decisions are PERMANENT by default
→ Personal preferences decay when the person changes roles

THE TRIAGE PIPELINE:
New knowledge enters

This is AGENTIC. The LLM is an actor. Not just a reader.
