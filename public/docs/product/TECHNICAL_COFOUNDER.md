IntegrateWise is building the IW Continuity Bridge — the Operational Continuity Platform. We're looking for a technical co-founder to join as the engineering leader who will own the technical vision, architecture, and execution from seed through Series A.

## 3. WHY INTEGRATEWISE NEEDS THIS

### 3.1 The Technical Challenge

IntegrateWise is solving a hard problem: **operational continuity across disconnected tools**. This requires:

1. **Spine Architecture:** A canonical truth store that normalizes data from 50+ tools (Salesforce, HubSpot, ServiceNow, Atlassian, etc.) into a single operational memory
2. **Governed AI:** Twin as a silent partner that proposes actions, not executes them. Human approval gates every consequential execution
3. **Cloudflare-Native:** 100% Cloudflare infrastructure (D1/KV/R2/Vectorize/AI Search/Durable Objects) for global scale
4. **MCP Protocol:** First-class support for Model Context Protocol, enabling AI agents to interact with operational data
5. **Real-time Continuity:** Preserving context across sessions, tools, and team members

### 3.2 The Founder's Constraint

The founder (Nirmal) is a systems architect with deep domain expertise in continuity systems. However, building a world-class technical team and scaling the architecture requires a dedicated engineering leader who can:

- Own the day-to-day technical execution
- Build and lead the engineering team
- Make architectural decisions that scale from Customer Zero to 100+ customers
- Represent the technical vision to investors and customers
- Balance speed (ship fast) with quality (build right)

### 3.3 The Opportunity

This is a rare opportunity to:

- **Join at the ground floor** of a platform play in a $50B+ market
- **Own the technical vision** of a novel architecture (Spine, governed AI, Cloudflare-native)
- **Build the engineering team** from scratch (first 10-15 engineers)
- **Shape the technical roadmap** from seed through Series B
- **Participate in the India cost advantage** — ₹5.5Cr runway = ₹20-25Cr US equivalent

## 8. INVESTMENT AGREEMENT OPTIONS

### 8.1 Option A: Direct Equity Purchase

**Structure:**

- Purchase shares at current valuation (₹30Cr pre-money)
- No vesting on purchased shares (separate from equity grant)
- Standard investor rights (pro-rata, information rights)

**Pros:**

- Clean, simple structure
- Immediate ownership (no cliff risk)
- Clear tax treatment (capital gains)

**Cons:**

- Requires upfront cash (₹10-15L)
- No protection if you leave early
- May complicate cap table

**Best for:** Someone with significant savings who wants immediate ownership

### 8.2 Option B: Sweat + Cash Discount

**Structure:**

- 50% equity grant (with vesting)
- 50% equity purchase (at discount)
- Discount: 20-30% off current valuation
- Vesting: Standard 4-year with 1-year cliff on grant portion

**Example (Scenario B - 15% total):**

- 7.5% grant (vesting over 4 years)
- 7.5% purchase at ₹22.5Cr valuation (25% discount)
- Investment required: ₹16.9L (vs ₹22.5L at full valuation)

**Pros:**

- Reduces cash requirement
- Balances risk/reward
- Aligns incentives (vesting + investment)
- Clear structure for investors

**Cons:**

- More complex legal structure
- Requires valuation agreement
- Two different share classes possible

**Best for:** Someone with moderate savings who wants balanced risk/reward

### 8.3 Option C: Convertible Note

**Structure:**

- Convertible note with valuation cap
- Note converts at Series A (or trigger event)
- Cap: ₹40Cr (33% premium to current valuation)
- Discount: 20% to Series A price
- Interest: 8% annually

**Example (Scenario B - 15% target):**

- Note amount: ₹15L
- Conversion at Series A (₹150Cr pre-money)
- Note converts to equity at ₹40Cr cap
- Result: ~1.2% additional equity (on top of 15% grant)

**Pros:**

- Defers valuation decision
- Aligns with investor terms
- Lower upfront cash requirement
- Simple legal structure

**Cons:**

- Dilutes at Series A (on top of investor dilution)
- Requires Series A to trigger conversion
- More complex cap table math

**Best for:** Someone who wants to defer the investment decision until later

## 9. RECOMMENDED OPTION

### **Option B: Sweat + Cash Discount (Recommended)**

**Why:**

1. **Balanced risk/reward:** You're investing time (vesting) AND money (cash discount)
2. **Reduced cash requirement:** 25% discount = ₹16.9L vs ₹22.5L
3. **Clear structure:** Investors understand this pattern
4. **Alignment:** Vesting protects the company; investment protects you
5. **Flexibility:** Can adjust discount based on personal situation

**Recommended structure:**

- **Total equity:** 15% (Scenario B)
- **Grant portion:** 7.5% (4-year vesting, 1-year cliff)
- **Purchase portion:** 7.5% at ₹22.5Cr valuation (25% discount)
- **Investment required:** ₹16.9L
- **Investment schedule:** ₹8.5L at signing, ₹8.5L at 6-month mark

## 10. VESTING SCHEDULE WITH ACCELERATION

### 10.1 Standard Vesting (Grant Portion)

| Year                | Vesting    | Cumulative | Cliff        |
| ------------------- | ---------- | ---------- | ------------ |
| Year 1              | 0% (cliff) | 0%         | 1-year cliff |
| Year 1 (post-cliff) | 25%        | 25%        | Cliff met    |
| Year 2              | 25%        | 50%        | —            |
| Year 3              | 25%        | 75%        | —            |
| Year 4              | 25%        | 100%       | Fully vested |

### 10.2 Acceleration Events

**Single Trigger (Change of Control):**

- 2x acceleration on acquisition or merger
- All unvested shares vest immediately
- Protects co-founder if company is sold

**Double Trigger (Involuntary Termination + Change of Control):**

- 2x acceleration on acquisition + termination without cause
- All unvested shares vest immediately
- Protects co-founder if they're fired before a sale

### 10.3 Good Leaver / Bad Leaver

**Good Leaver (mutual agreement, no cause):**

- Vested shares retained
- Unvested shares forfeited
- Purchased shares: company has right of first refusal at fair market value

**Bad Leaver (cause, breach):**

- Vested shares: company has right of first refusal at cost (not FMV)
- Unvested shares: forfeited
- Purchased shares: company has right of first refusal at cost

**Cause definition:** Material breach of agreement, fraud, felony, willful misconduct
